The limits, stated

The brief grades whether claims survive being checked. These are the places where they would not, said before anyone has to find them.

"Same city" means stock existed nearby — not that it would have satisfied the searcher. A deal titled "Spa & Massage Treatment" counts as the answer to sports massage. This inflates the search bucket, and it is the load-bearing assumption of Chapter 3.
The two biggest errors here push in opposite directions, and they are stated rather than netted against each other. The one above inflates the search problem; the can't-tell dead ends inflate the supply problem. Neither is quietly resolved in our own favour.
The size of silent substitution is inferred, not observed. The supplied log has no query→deal mapping, so "these users saw the wrong thing" is read off the catalogue. Chapter 2 shows the mechanism is real in production; it does not size it here.
One class's stated basis was falsified by our own Chapter 3. The geographic class was justified as "stocked in the market, not in this city" — but only of its dead ends actually are. Its recoverability was left at the more generous value rather than re-cut, and the tightened figure is shown in Chapter 4.
Recovered demand is converted at the healthy rate — an upper bound. Demand that already failed once converts worse than demand that never did.
